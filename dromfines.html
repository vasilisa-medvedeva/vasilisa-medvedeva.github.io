Drom Fines · iOS / Android

Drom Fines lets drivers check and pay traffic fines online.

Fine data comes straight from the official government database (GIS GMP). Paying in the app gives drivers legally valid confirmation and helps them avoid late fees.

App Store Google Play

My Role and Responsibilities

The team: a product manager, an analyst, two iOS and two Android engineers, and a QA engineer, with backend owned by a separate team.

  • Owned UX for the fines app on iOS and Android: registration, fine search, payment, and onboarding.
  • Redesigned the payment screen around saved cards, cutting manual input for repeat payers.
  • Designed and A/B-tested the Lottie onboarding that brought 16% more registrations.
  • Moved the fines feature into the main Drom app, restyling it on Drom’s design system and dark theme.
  • Mapped the flow for fines tied to a driver’s license rather than a vehicle.
  • Prepared the onboarding animations as Lottie files for development.
Case 01

Onboarding

Problem

A significant share of new users never finished registration: they opened the app once and never came back. The first thing the app asked for was documents, before it had shown any reason to trust it with them.

Solution

My hypothesis: people dropped off because nothing explained why the app was worth their documents. I designed a short onboarding on animated Lottie screens with two messages: the data is official, straight from GIBDD, the Federal Treasury, MADI, AMPP, and FSSP, and checking takes one scan of the registration certificate.

We ran it as a month-long A/B test: the new onboarding against the flow without one.

Outcome

  • The onboarding variant brought 16% more registrations
Case 02

Registration Screen Redesign

Problem

The app could scan documents instead of asking users to type the plate and registration certificate numbers, but almost nobody found it: scanning hid behind a small camera icon inside an input field. Most users took the slowest path through the very first screen of the product.

The input fields themselves looked dated and gave little feedback while typing.

Solution

I rebuilt the hierarchy: scanning became the primary button at the top of the screen, manual fields the fallback below it. The inputs got clear labels and visible states.

The layout also reserves room for registering by driver’s license, which later became its own case.

Outcome

  • After the redesign, registration drop-off fell by 20%
  • Scanning is now the first thing a new user sees; manual entry became the fallback
  • The same layout later absorbed the driver’s license scenario without a rework
Case 03

Payment Screen Redesign

Problem

Every payment started from scratch: the app had no saved cards and no Apple Pay or Google Pay, so users retyped card details for every fine. Repeat payers, the core of a revenue-critical flow, felt it most.

The screen also looked different from the rest of the app, and it looked different at the worst possible moment: when users hand over money.

Solution

I redesigned the screen around saved cards: a returning user confirms the payment instead of retyping the card. Usability testing validated the new layout before release.

The visual style now matches the rest of the app, and the payment-method block has room for Apple Pay and Google Pay, so adding them won’t need a redesign.

Outcome

  • After the redesign, payment conversion doubled and fines revenue grew 5×
  • Repeat payments went from retyping a card to confirming a saved one
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay can be added without a redesign
Case 04

Integration into the Drom Ecosystem

Problem

The fines feature was moving into the main Drom app, where millions of users already knew Drom’s visual language, including its dark theme. Dropped in as-is, it would have read as a third-party embed inside a trusted product.

Solution

I restyled the feature on Drom’s design system: its dark palette, its components, its navigation patterns. Existing Drom components were reused wherever possible instead of building a parallel kit.

A custom onboarding animation in Drom’s style introduced fines to the existing audience.

Outcome

  • The fines feature reads as native Drom, down to the dark theme
  • Reusing Drom components meant no parallel UI kit to maintain
Case 05

Driver’s License Fine Tracking

Problem

Some fines are issued to a driver’s license rather than a vehicle, so the app, which tracked fines only by car, silently showed users an incomplete list. A “no fines” screen could be wrong.

Solution

I mapped the full flow for adding and tracking fines by license and designed the license card for the fines list. A banner in the list prompts drivers to add their license, so the feature surfaces exactly where the incomplete list lives.
